# What Is the Water Method for Skin?

The “water method” is a simple way to apply hydrating skincare: leave skin slightly damp after cleansing, then apply a hydrating serum or essence and moisturizer. You can use products you already own. The point is to help skin hold on to moisture, not to add a long new routine. Strong retinoids and exfoliating acids are a different story: follow their instructions and generally apply them to fully dry skin if they tend to irritate you.

## What is the water method?

Dermatologist Dendy Engelman describes the method in [ELLE's explainer](https://www.elle.com/beauty/a74279732/the-water-method-skincare-routine-explained/): pat away excess water after washing, then put hydrating products on while skin is damp, before moisturizer. The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin) likewise advises people with dry skin to moisturize while skin is still damp after bathing.

This is an application technique, not a new treatment with a proven effect size. It may help skin feel more comfortable and look temporarily smoother, especially when indoor heating leaves it tight.

## The three-step version

- **Cleanse gently.** Use lukewarm water and pat until your face is damp, not dripping. Avoid rubbing with the towel.

- **Add hydration if you have it.** A simple serum or essence with ingredients such as glycerin or hyaluronic acid can go on now. Skip this step if you do not own one.

- **Moisturize right away.** Use a moisturizer that suits your skin. In the morning, finish with sunscreen.

The AAD also suggests short warm showers rather than long hot ones for dry skin. For seasonal adjustments, see our [fall skincare reset](https://skincare.allwomenstalk.com/skincare-tips-for-fall/).

## Water method versus skin flooding

Skin flooding

Apply hydrating products to slightly damp skin
Layer several hydrating products, often including mist, essence, serum and moisturizer

Can be as simple as moisturizer after cleansing
Usually a longer routine

No new product is required
Extra layers are optional, not necessary for everyone

Engelman makes this distinction in ELLE. If a short routine works for you, there is no reason to add layers just because a trend has a name.

## What should go on dry skin instead?

Engelman cautions that damp skin can increase penetration and irritation from strong retinoids, exfoliating acids and similar treatments. Unless a product's instructions say otherwise, let skin dry fully before using a potentially irritating active. If you use a retinoid at night, our [nighttime skincare order guide](https://skincare.allwomenstalk.com/order-to-apply-your-skincare-products/) can help you map the rest of the routine.

Do not assume you must exfoliate before hydrating or use toner to remove minerals from tap water. Those extra steps are not necessary to try the basic method, and too much exfoliation can irritate skin. If you have a skin condition or are unsure how to use a prescribed product, ask your dermatologist.

## Adjust it to your skin

If your skin is…
Try…

Dry or tight
A comfortable moisturizer on slightly damp skin; choose a richer formula if needed.

Oily or acne-prone
A lightweight, non-comedogenic moisturizer. Hydration does not require a heavy cream.

Sensitive or easily irritated
A short, fragrance-free routine and fewer active ingredients. Stop if a product stings or worsens irritation.

## FAQ

### What is the water method in skincare?

It means applying hydrating products while skin is slightly damp, then finishing with moisturizer.

### Should moisturizer go on damp or dry skin?

For dry skin, the AAD recommends applying moisturizer while skin is still damp after bathing. Choose a formula your skin tolerates.

### Is the water method the same as skin flooding?

No. Skin flooding layers several hydrating products. The water method refers to applying products to damp skin and can be much simpler.

### Can I put retinol or acids on damp skin?

Follow your product or prescriber's directions. Potentially irritating retinoids and exfoliating acids are generally better applied to fully dry skin unless instructions say otherwise.
