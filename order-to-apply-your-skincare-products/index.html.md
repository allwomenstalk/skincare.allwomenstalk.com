# The Right Nighttime Skincare Routine Order: Three Steps Is Plenty

A simple nighttime skincare routine has three places: cleanse, apply a treatment if you use one, then moisturize. You can add products for a specific reason, but you do not need an eight-step shelf to care for your skin. The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/apply-skin-care-certain-order/) puts medication or treatment after cleansing and moisturizer after that.

## The basic nighttime order

### 1. Cleanse

Use a gentle cleanser to remove the day's sunscreen, makeup and oil, then pat your skin dry. If heavy makeup needs a separate remover first, use one; if a single cleanse works, there is no prize for doing two. Cleveland Clinic dermatologist Sean McGregor [cautions](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/proper-skin-care-product-order) that double cleansing can sometimes do more harm than good. Our [double-cleansing guide](https://skincare.allwomenstalk.com/the-step-by-step-guide-on-how-to-double-cleanse/) explains the method if you use it, but watch how your own skin responds.

### 2. Treat, if needed

This is the slot for a prescribed acne medication, a retinoid or one targeted serum. Follow a prescription and its label rather than a generic layering chart. If you are unsure which product belongs first, ask your dermatologist or pharmacist. More active products at once can mean more irritation, especially when you are starting something new.

### 3. Moisturize

Finish with a moisturizer that feels comfortable on your skin. It helps limit water loss and can be especially useful when a treatment dries or irritates your skin. Oily and acne-prone skin still needs hydration; choose a texture you like instead of skipping this step solely because your skin produces oil.

For most people, that is enough. [Cosmopolitan's interview with dermatologist Hallie McDonald](https://www.cosmopolitan.com/style-beauty/beauty/a74263657/nighttime-skincare-routine/) likewise recommends a short routine and warns that adding products increases the chances of irritation. A rough layering guide is lighter textures before heavier ones. You usually need only let a layer absorb, not run a timer between every step.

The [Cleveland Clinic guide](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/proper-skin-care-product-order) places face oil just before moisturizer, while some products advise it last. Check the directions on the product you own; there is no universal oil rule. The same guide treats toner and eye cream as optional. If your routine feels comfortable and your clinician has not given you a different order, keep it simple.

## Where does retinol go at night?

A retinoid or retinol usually takes the treatment slot after cleansing and before moisturizer. The [AAD's retinoid guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/retinoid-retinol) advises starting with a lower-strength product every other night and building up as tolerated. Use sun protection during the day. Ask a dermatologist before starting if you have significant redness or inflammation, and watch for irritation that can leave dark marks, particularly on skin of color.

Do not use retinoids during pregnancy; discuss any medication or pregnancy plans with your clinician. If you are using acne medication or exfoliating acids too, ask a dermatologist how to combine them rather than piling several strong treatments into one evening. A prescribed plan takes precedence over this general order.

## Do oily or acne-prone skin types need moisturizer?

Yes. The old version of this page suggested skipping moisturizer for oily or acne-prone skin; that advice has been removed. Oil and hydration are different. A lightweight moisturizer may feel better than a rich cream, but producing oil is not a reason to leave the skin unprotected after cleansing or treatment. If every moisturizer stings or seems to worsen acne, a dermatologist can help you choose one.

## Why fewer products can be better

The AAD warns that using too many products, especially several products aimed at the same concern, may irritate skin. A shorter routine also makes it easier to notice which product is helping and which one is causing trouble. You can enjoy a longer ritual if your skin tolerates it, but it is optional. Our [10-step Korean skincare guide](https://skincare.allwomenstalk.com/the-step-korean-skincare-routine/) shows a different approach; the best routine is one that fits your skin and that you can use consistently.

## Three sample nighttime routines

- **Basic:** gentle cleanser, then moisturizer.

- **Acne-prone:** gentle cleanser, one acne treatment recommended for you, then a lightweight moisturizer.

- **New to retinol:** gentle cleanser, retinol on the schedule advised for your skin, then moisturizer; keep daytime sun protection in place.

These are examples, not personalized treatment plans. If you have persistent acne, eczema, rosacea, a pregnancy question or significant irritation, ask a dermatologist before adding actives.

## FAQ

### How many steps should a nighttime routine have?

Two or three are enough for many people: cleanse, treat if needed, moisturize. Add only products that serve a clear purpose for your skin.

### Does retinol go before or after moisturizer?

Often after cleansing and before moisturizer, unless your clinician or product instructions tell you otherwise.

### Where does eye cream go?

If you use one, place it after treatment or serum and before face moisturizer. It is optional for many people.

### Should oily skin skip moisturizer?

No. Choose a comfortable lightweight formula rather than skipping hydration because your skin is oily.
