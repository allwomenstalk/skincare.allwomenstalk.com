# 7 Signs You Need to See a Dermatologist \(And When to Make an Appointment\)

There are many clear signs you need to see a dermatologist, and each one is a compelling reason to make an appointment without delay. Postponing a visit can leave you feeling uncomfortable or in pain, and in some situations, it can lead to health complications that are much harder to address down the road. According to guidelines from the [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org), a board-certified dermatologist is trained to evaluate a vast range of skin conditions with precise diagnostic tools and targeted treatment plans. Ignoring persistent skin issues rarely pays off when expert care is readily available. Take a look at these key warning signs, then grab your phone to schedule an evaluation with a skin specialist.

## 1. Actinic Keratoses

This condition typically appears as rough, red, or scaly patches on sun-exposed areas of the body. While actinic keratoses are frequently associated with older adults, they can develop in younger individuals who have accumulated sun exposure over time. This is one of the most critical reasons to visit a dermatologist because these rough spots are considered precancerous lesions. While not every unusual spot on your skin poses a threat, having them evaluated regularly ensures that any potential issues are identified and treated at an early, manageable stage.

## 2. Growing Mole

Most moles on your body are completely harmless and will maintain the same size, color, and shape throughout your life. However, when a mole begins to transform, it requires immediate professional attention. A changing mole can be an early indicator of melanoma or other forms of skin cancer. Resources from the [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org) emphasize that when caught in its earliest stages, most skin cancers are highly treatable. Delaying an examination allows abnormal cells to grow deeper or spread, making successful treatment significantly more challenging.

| Symptom | Potential Concern | Recommended Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rough, scaly patches | Actinic Keratoses | Dermatologist evaluation |
| Evolving mole shape | Melanoma risk | Skin biopsy & checkup |
| Persistent blemish | Skin tissue changes | Professional diagnosis |

Monitoring your body for new or evolving marks is essential to maintaining long-term skin health. If you notice spots that resemble [actinic keratoses](#1) or spots undergoing rapid changes in texture, schedule a professional skin examination promptly.

## 3. Unhealing Wounds

A wound or sore that refuses to heal is another major sign that something underlying needs attention. Medical experts advise that if you have a cut, scrape, or persistent blemish that fails to resolve over several weeks, or one that heals only to recur in the exact same spot, you should see a doctor right away. Non-healing lesions can be a sign of basal cell or squamous cell carcinoma, and getting a prompt diagnosis prevents further tissue damage.

## 4. Changing Spots

Hardly anyone has completely flawless skin without a single freckle, mark, or mole. Because natural variations are normal, it is important to become familiar with your baseline skin pattern so you notice subtle updates quickly. If you observe changes in color, irregular borders, or a sudden increase in size, have those areas examined by a skin specialist. Comparing suspicious spots to existing lesions like [growing moles](#2) can help you stay vigilant between routine checkups.

## 5. Unresponsive Acne

Occasional breakouts affect people of all ages, but chronic or painful acne that fails to respond to over-the-counter treatments warrants a visit to the doctor. A dermatologist can determine the root cause of your breakouts, whether hormonal, bacterial, or inflammatory, and adjust your treatment regimen accordingly. Clinical care guidelines from the [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org) note that prescription-strength topicals or oral medications are often necessary for persistent cases.

You should consider seeing a specialist for your breakouts if you experience any of the following signs:

- Painful, deep cystic nodules beneath the skin surface
- Persistent breakouts that lead to permanent pitting or severe scarring
- No noticeable improvement after using over-the-counter products consistently for 8 to 12 weeks

## 6. Weird New Skin Issues

If you notice any novel, unfamiliar, or unexplained symptoms on your skin, having a physician evaluate the area is the safest approach. You might be experiencing adult-onset eczema, contact dermatitis, psoriasis, or an allergic reaction to a cosmetic product or environmental trigger. A dermatologist can perform diagnostic tests to identify the precise underlying issue so you can begin targeted therapy, which is far more effective than self-diagnosing or managing [unresponsive acne](#5) or mysterious rashes on your own.

## 7. Scars

If you have sustained a traumatic injury, undergone surgery, or suffered severe skin inflammation that resulted in prominent scarring, a dermatologist can help improve the area's appearance. Modern dermatology offers numerous advanced techniques, including laser therapy, microneedling, and topical silicone treatments, to minimize noticeable scar tissue and boost your comfort. In addition, your doctor can monitor the healing process over time to ensure the tissue stays healthy and free of complications.

Why do you typically schedule visits with your dermatologist? Many individuals schedule an annual full-body skin check as part of their preventative health routine. If you have noticed any of these warning signs, reach out to a healthcare professional today to keep your skin protected and healthy.
