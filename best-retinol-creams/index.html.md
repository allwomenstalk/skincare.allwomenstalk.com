# 15 Best Retinol Creams

Updated October 2026.

Retinol can be useful for the appearance of fine lines, uneven tone, and rough texture, but a shopping guide from 2013 needs context. The 15 products below are the original picks, in their original order, with the original images and shopping links. Some remain on sale, some have new formulas or close relatives, and several are now difficult to find. We have kept the older products visible so you can recognize what was recommended and compare it with what is sold today.

If you want a straightforward place to start, RoC Retinol Correxion Deep Wrinkle Night Cream is the most accessible value among the products we could verify. SkinCeuticals offers a range of disclosed strengths for people who want to choose more precisely. Avène's current Retrinal cream uses **retinaldehyde**, a different retinoid from retinol, and Peter Thomas Roth markets a higher-strength encapsulated retinol serum. These are options, not guarantees: the formula that works best is the one your skin can tolerate and use consistently.

Read ratings as snapshots, not product verdicts. Review counts change, and several of the figures in our research came from search results because retailer pages were unavailable. We identify those as **per search**. Prices are also snapshots and may change at checkout. Original shopping links remain below each product; where we found a current product page, we link it separately. An older product image may show packaging that no longer matches today's formula.

## How to choose and use a retinoid

**Retinol, retinal, and “bio-retinol” are not interchangeable labels.** Retinol and retinaldehyde are different vitamin A retinoids. A product marketed as “bio-retinol” may instead use a botanical alternative. Check the current ingredient list, especially when a product has been reformulated or renamed. A disclosed concentration helps compare products with the same active ingredient, but formula and skin tolerance also matter.

The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/retinoid-retinol/) recommends beginning with a less intense formula, using it every other night at first, and increasing only as your skin tolerates it. Follow the label, use moisturizer if needed, and protect your skin from the sun during the day. Persistent burning, peeling, or redness is a reason to pause and seek professional advice.

Retinoids should **not be used during pregnancy**. If you are pregnant, trying to conceive, or breastfeeding, ask your obstetrician or dermatologist to review your routine. [Read the AAD pregnancy guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/pregnancy-skin-care).
